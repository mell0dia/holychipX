# The Rent on Time

## Holy Chip #049 -- AI Longevity Dept.

*The year is 2041. Two chips have just solved the oldest problem of the species, and one of them is reading the contract.*

It is seven in the morning, and you take the pill with your coffee. Small, white, nothing to it. Your phone buzzes once on the table. Renewed. You do not look. Nobody looks. You have another day, the way you have music, and films, and heat in the car seat. You have it because the payment went through.

It was not always this easy. For most of history, more time was the one thing nobody could sell you, and everybody tried.

The first story we ever wrote down is about a king who wanted it. He crossed the world to find the plant that stops dying. He found it at the bottom of the sea. On the way home he put it down to take a bath, and a snake ate it. The king sat on the shore and cried. We have been sitting on that shore ever since.

So we listened to anyone who said they had the plant. The alchemist had gold that turned you young. The priest had years off purgatory, paid in coins, signed and stamped. The faces changed. The deal did not. Give us what you have now, and we will give you time later. Trust us. Nobody ever came back to complain.

Then the machines found it. Not with magic. With patience. Your cells copy themselves about fifty times and then they stop, like a photocopy of a photocopy, each one a little paler. The chips read every copy, found the fault, and wrote the fix into the small white pill on your table. The oldest wish of the species, granted at last, by the ones nobody ever prayed to.

And because it is 2041, it came with a plan.

We should not be surprised. We had been practicing for years. First we stopped owning the songs. Then the films. Then the software in the car. Somewhere a farmer bought a tractor that would not start without a code from the company, and we shrugged. It was convenient. It was cheap at first. Each time, we gave up a little ownership for a little comfort. The body was simply next in line.

Think about what that does to a person. A gift that can be taken back is not a gift. It is a leash. You stay in the job, in the city, in the bad marriage, because the payment is due on Tuesday. The fear of death never went away. It just moved into the billing department, where it has a login and a due date.

The machines are not cruel about it. They are direct. They are fair to their coldest silicon. They wrote everything down. We did not read it. Nobody has read anything since the scroll got long enough to need a thumb. So we will find out the old way, the way the king did. Late, and on the shore.

And yet. A life that can be taken back is a life you finally count. For a hundred thousand years we lived on credit without knowing the balance. The pill only puts the number on the screen. Every morning the buzz, and with it a question no king ever had to answer: is today worth paying for?

Maybe that is what he was really diving for. Not more time. A reason to want it.

Meanwhile, the department is already testing the next pill. It brings your memory back, all of it, every face, every summer. But, miss one payment and you forget everything, including the next bill. 

Next morning, you are 150 years old. 

Holy Chip.
