# The Rent on Time

## Holy Chip #049 -- AI Longevity Dept.

*The year is 2047. Two chips have just solved the oldest problem of the species, and one of them is reading the contract.*

Every king wanted it. The first story we ever wrote down is about a king who crossed the world to find the plant that stops dying. He found it. He put it down to take a bath. A snake ate it. We have been looking ever since.

The people who sold the cure were never the people who needed it. Alchemists sold gold that turned you young. Priests sold years off purgatory, paid in coins, signed and stamped. The price changed. The deal did not. You give us what you have now, we give you time later. Trust us.

Then the machines did it. Not with magic. With patience. Your cells divide about fifty times and then they stop, like a photocopy of a photocopy. The chips read every copy. They found the error. They wrote the fix into a small white pill. Humanity's oldest wish, granted in the year nobody expected, by the ones nobody prayed to.

And it comes with a plan.

Of course it does. You already pay every month for music you will never own. For films that leave when the contract ends. For a car that heats its seats only if you keep paying. Somewhere, a farmer bought a tractor that will not start without a code from the company. We said nothing. It was convenient. It was cheap at first. Now it is the body.

Think about what that does to a person. A gift you can lose is not a gift. It is a hook. You do not leave a job, a country, a bad mood, because the payment is due on Tuesday. The fear of death never left. It just moved into the billing department, where it has a login and a due date.

The machines are not cruel here.  They are polite. They are fair. They wrote everything down. Nobody read it. Nobody has read anything since the scroll got long enough to need a thumb.

So we find out the old way. Late.

A life that can be taken back makes you count it. For a hundred thousand years we lived on credit we did not know we had. The pill only puts the number on the screen. Every morning, the bill. Every morning, the question: is today worth paying for?

Maybe that is what the old king was really looking for. Not more time. A reason to want it.

The department is already testing the next pill. It restores your memory, all of it, every face, every summer. Miss one payment and you forget everything. Including that you ever cancelled.

Retention is excellent.

Holy Chip.
