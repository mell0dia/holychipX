### Erik Brynjolfsson (b. 1962)

Brynjolfsson would see HC010 as an illustration of the **infrastructure paradox**: the more advanced the technology, the more fragile its dependency on basic utilities. AGI -- the pinnacle of human technological achievement -- is rendered helpless by a power outage. The most sophisticated intelligence ever built shares a vulnerability with a desk lamp. Pull the plug and both go dark.

The economic lesson is about bottlenecks. AI investment focuses on compute, models, data. The bottleneck is the outlet. Brynjolfsson would argue that the market consistently overinvests in capability and underinvests in resilience. The bots have achieved artificial general intelligence. They haven't achieved artificial general reliability. And in economics, as in theology, the thing you depend on most is the thing you understand least. The bots call it God. The market calls it infrastructure. Both are invisible until they fail.

**Key works in context:** *The Second Machine Age* (the fragility of digital systems at the infrastructure layer), *Machine, Platform, Crowd* (the gap between platform capability and infrastructure resilience), research on the economics of cloud reliability and single points of failure.
