Acemoglu would see HC004 as the purest distillation of his thesis in *Power and Progress*: the people who deploy technology are not immune to being displaced by it. Throughout history, elites have adopted new technologies believing they would consolidate their power. Often, those technologies restructured power entirely. The economic logic is stark: if AI agents can perform executive functions -- strategy, resource allocation, organizational design -- then the C-suite is a cost center, not a value creator. Acemoglu would note that this disruption flows upward, not downward. We worry about AI replacing factory workers and truck drivers. HC004 suggests the corner office goes first. The irony is structural: the people with the power to deploy AI are the people most confident they won't be replaced by it. Acemoglu has a word for this confidence. He calls it a trap.

**Key works in context:** *Power and Progress* (technology displacing its own deployers), *Why Nations Fail* (elites undone by the tools of their own power), research on AI and executive labor market disruption.
